When Tipping Is NOT Expected

Business Owners vs. Employees

Owners set their own pricing. Tipping applies primarily to employees who don't control their rates.

Service Charge vs. Gratuity

NOT the same thing. A service charge may go to the company, not the staff. Ask: "Does this fee go to the staff?"

Tips Never Expected

Brief deliveries. Poor service (address the issue instead). When budget doesn't allow — reviews and referrals are meaningful alternatives.

How to Deliver Tips

Labeled Envelope System

Withdraw cash 2 weeks before. Prepare envelopes with vendor name + thank-you note. Seal and organize in delivery order.

The "Tip Distributor"

Designate ONE person (planner, MOH, best man). Provide checklist with names, envelopes, and timing. The couple should never handle envelopes on the day.

Day-Of Vendor Communication

Point of Contact

Planner becomes sole vendor contact. If no planner, designate a liaison. The couple should NOT field vendor questions on the day.

Vendor Meals

Any vendor working 4-6+ hours during meal service gets a hot meal. Serve after couple but before remaining tables. Provide a private dining area.

Timeline & Confirmation

Share final timeline 3-4 weeks before. Confirmation call 1 week before. Reconfirm arrival times, setup, and any changes.

When Things Go Wrong

Address Immediately If:

Safety issue. Contracted services not being delivered. Problem will worsen if unaddressed. Have your planner or liaison handle it.

Let It Go If:

Minor aesthetic issue only you notice. Small delay that won't cascade. Imperfection won't show in photos or affect guest experience.

Post-Wedding

Wait 2-3 weeks. Contact vendor directly before posting reviews. Be factual about what happened vs. what was contracted.

Overall Tipping Budget

Budget 5-10% of total wedding cost for tips. Typical total: $500-$1,500.
