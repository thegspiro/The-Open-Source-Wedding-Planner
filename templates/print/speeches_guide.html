Everything your speakers need to know about timing, structure, and delivery

Speech Order & Timing

Traditional Order

Best Man → Maid of Honor → Father of the Bride → Father of the Groom → The Couple. MC or DJ introduces each speaker.

Modern Variations

Brides speaking more, mothers included, joint speeches, flexible order. Do what fits your celebration.

Ideal Speech Lengths

Speaker Ideal Length Max
Best Man 3-5 min 7 min
Maid of Honor 3-5 min 7 min
Father of the Bride 5-7 min 9 min
Parents of the Groom 3-5 min 7 min
Couple's Toast 2-3 min 5 min

Total speech block: aim for under 30 minutes.

⚠ What Happens When Speeches Run Long

Audience attention drops after 3 min per speaker. Caterers face delays. Guests get hungry. Dancing time gets cut. Keep it tight.

Speech Structure

The 3-Part Framework

Opening (30 sec - 1 min)

Introduce yourself, establish your relationship to the couple, and hook the audience. You have about 15 seconds to earn their attention.

Body (2-3 min)

One or two anecdotes that reveal something meaningful, then bridge to the couple. One great story beats three mediocre ones.

Closing (30 sec - 1 min)

"Please raise your glasses..." Name both partners. Specific wish. Raise glass toward couple. Sip.

Good Openers

  • A brief, funny observation about the couple or the day
  • "For those who don't know me..." followed by a quick, warm intro
  • A short, engaging anecdote that draws the audience in

Bad Openers

  • "Webster's dictionary defines..." — overused, instantly loses the room
  • "I'm really nervous" — makes the audience nervous too
  • "I didn't prepare anything" — tells guests you don't care enough

What to Include

Personal Stories That Reveal Character

Choose stories that show who this person really is. One great story told well beats three rushed through.

Humor, Balanced with Sentiment

Self-deprecating humor works well. Test jokes on someone outside the wedding party first.

Compliment Both Partners

Even if you primarily know one half, acknowledge and welcome the other. This is about their union.

Absent Loved Ones

Get couple's approval first. Keep it uplifting. Pivot to how proud they'd be. Brief and warm.

What to Avoid

Speech Don'ts
  • ✗ Ex-partners — NEVER, not even as a joke
  • ✗ Embarrassing stories that would mortify parents or grandparents
  • ✗ Inside jokes nobody outside your group understands
  • ✗ Making it about yourself — you are the messenger, not the story
  • ✗ Excessive alcohol before speaking — one drink max
  • ✗ Reading entirely from your phone — use printed cue cards
  • ✗ Winging it — always prepare
  • ✗ Divorce statistics or cynical marriage jokes

Tips for Nervous Speakers

Preparation Timeline

  • 4-6 weeks out: Brainstorm stories and themes
  • 3-4 weeks out: Write your first draft
  • 2 weeks out: Edit and test on a trusted friend
  • 1 week out: Finalize cue cards, practice daily
  • Day before: One full run-through, then put it away
  • Day of: One quiet read-through, then trust your prep

Cue Cards

Bullet points, not memorized, not full script. Number your cards. Large, clear text. Give a backup copy to a friend.

Breathing Techniques

Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)

Inhale 4 counts, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4. Repeat 3-4 times.

Extended Exhale (4-4-8)

Inhale 4 counts, hold 4, exhale slowly for 8. Activates parasympathetic nervous system. Practice 5-10 min before speech.

Where to Look

Scan left, center, right. Find 3-5 friendly smiling faces. Look at the couple during heartfelt moments.

Toast Etiquette for Guests

Raising Your Glass

Only raise when the speaker invites you. Hold by the stem. Raise toward the couple.

The Couple Being Toasted

The couple remains seated and does not drink to themselves. Smile and acknowledge the toast.

Non-Drinkers

Absolutely participate — raise whatever you have. Water, juice, soda. Any beverage is acceptable.

When During the Reception

Best between courses (after main, before dessert) or after dinner before dancing begins.
