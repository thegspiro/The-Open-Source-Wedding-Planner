Making guests feel welcome and expressing gratitude

Pre-Wedding Hospitality

Welcome Bags

Include water, local snacks, a weekend itinerary, a hangover kit, and a handwritten note. Deliver to the hotel for check-in. One bag per room or per couple.

Welcome Party

Host the evening before, keep it to 2-3 hours. Everyone invited to the wedding should be invited. Casual format: appetizers and drinks are sufficient. Not a gift occasion.

Rehearsal Dinner

Traditionally the groom's family hosts. Must invite: wedding party + partners, officiant + partner, parents, grandparents, and siblings. Never more formal than the wedding.

Day-Of Guest Experience

Ceremony Programs

Include order of ceremony, wedding party list, a thank-you message, and an optional "In Loving Memory" section. A few sincere lines of thanks go a long way.

Signage

Prepare signs for parking, welcome, directional arrows, restrooms, seating chart, and an unplugged ceremony notice. Clear signage prevents confusion and reduces questions.

Dietary Needs

Add a dietary field to your RSVP. Label buffet items (GF, V, VG, DF, NF). Brief catering staff on restrictions. Use coded place cards for plated meals.

Accessibility

Ensure wheelchair-accessible paths. Remove chairs for wheelchair users. Consider a sign language interpreter. Use high-contrast fonts. Add an accommodations field to your RSVP.

Post-Wedding Thank-You Notes

Timeline

Pre-wedding gifts: within 2 weeks. Shower hosts: within 2 days. Wedding gifts: within 3 months (not a year). Set a daily goal of 3-4 notes.

The Formula

Warm greeting, specific gift mention, personal connection (how you will use it or a shared memory), warm closing. This keeps notes sincere without overthinking.

Registry Gift

Name the specific item and how you will use it. "Thank you for the beautiful stand mixer. We have already used it twice."

Cash Gift

Mention your plans for the money but never state the amount. "Your generous gift is going toward our honeymoon trip to Portugal."

Group Gift

Each contributor receives their own individual note. Never send a single group thank-you.

No Gift

Focus on their attendance. "Having you there made the day so special. Your presence truly was the best gift."

Who Else to Thank

Parents (support and contributions), wedding party (pair with attendant gift), exceptional vendors, and anyone who helped with logistics.

Format

Always handwritten. No fill-in-the-blank cards, no emails, no social media posts as substitutes.

Guest Favors

Keep It Simple

Favors are optional. Edible favors work best: cookies, honey, coffee. Avoid overly personalized items guests rarely keep.

Donation in Lieu of Favors

Choose a meaningful cause. One discreet sign on the favor table. Keep the amount private. Do not say "in honor of our guests."

When Favors Are Not Needed

Already providing a generous experience, budget is tight, or hosting an intimate wedding with fewer than 30 guests.
