A guide to staying mindful, connecting with guests, and savoring every moment

Staying Present & Mindful

Unplug from Technology

Leave your phone with someone you trust. Resist the urge to check notifications or post to social media. Let your photographer and guests capture the moments for you.

Build Buffer Time into Your Timeline

Even 5-10 extra minutes between events gives you room to breathe and absorb the moment. An overpacked timeline is the number one enemy of being present.

Start the Day with a Grounding Ritual

Take a few minutes for deep breathing, meditation, or journaling. Inhale through your nose, hold, exhale through your mouth. This helps manage adrenaline and sets a peaceful tone.

Let Go of Perfectionism

Small things will go wrong. Embracing imperfections prevents stress from pulling you out of the moment. These unexpected moments often become your best stories.

Trust Your Vendor Team

Let your professionals handle logistics and troubleshoot. Your job today is to experience the day, not manage it.

Eat and Hydrate

The first place weddings go sideways is when the couple doesn't eat. Have a plan for lunch and keep water accessible throughout the day.

Private Moments as a Couple

Schedule Time Just for the Two of You

Carve out private moments after the ceremony, during sunset, or during the reception. These quiet pauses are often what couples remember most vividly.

Consider a First Look

A private first look gives you a calm, intimate connection point before the ceremony and frees up cocktail hour to spend with guests.

Exchange a Note or Small Gift

In the morning, exchange a handwritten note or small token. This anchors your connection before the whirlwind begins.

Check In with Each Other Throughout the Day

Hold hands, share a quick kiss, whisper something between events. You are a team. These small acts of connection keep you grounded.

Engaging with Your Guests

1 Receiving Line (Most Traditional)

Best for 75+ guests. Position at the ceremony exit or reception entrance. Keep it brief: "Thank you for being here" plus one personal comment. Have refreshments available for those waiting.

2 Table Visits During Dinner (Most Popular)

Spend 3-5 minutes per table while guests eat. Have your planner accompany you to manage timing. You can divide and conquer across different tables.

3 Cocktail Hour Greeting (Most Casual)

Mingle while guests enjoy drinks. Works best if portraits are done beforehand. If you miss a few people, catch them during the reception.

Making Guests Feel Welcome

Welcome Bags with Handwritten Notes

For out-of-town guests: include local snacks, a weekend itinerary, and your gratitude. People feel valued before the event even starts.

Thank-You Messages in the Program

A brief note in your ceremony program shows guests they matter. Even a few sincere lines go a long way.

Ask Guests to Unplug

A friendly sign or officiant announcement can remind guests to stay present and keep the ceremony distraction-free.

Host a Pre-Wedding Gathering

A welcome party gives you relaxed time with guests you might not reach on the busy wedding day. Especially valuable for out-of-town guests.

Day-Of Reminders Checklist

  • Hand your phone to someone you trust
  • Eat a real meal before the ceremony
  • Take three deep breaths before walking down the aisle
  • Look into your partner's eyes during your vows
  • Take a private 5-minute moment together after the ceremony
  • Greet and thank every guest (choose your approach)
  • Keep water nearby throughout the day
  • Step outside together for a quiet moment during the reception
  • Don't skip eating your reception dinner
  • Take one last look around the room before you leave
