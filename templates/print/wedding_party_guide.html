What each role handles before, during, and after the wedding

Maid / Matron of Honor

Pre-Wedding

Plan bridal shower (with bridesmaids contributing). Organize bachelorette party. Attend dress shopping and fittings. Coordinate bridesmaids on attire, schedules, logistics. Help with invitations, programs, favors.

Day-Of

Arrive early to bridal suite. Help bride dress (zip gown, secure veil, jewelry). Carry emergency kit (hairspray, bobby pins, sewing kit, stain remover, pain relievers, fashion tape). Keep bride calm, fed, hydrated, and on schedule.

Ceremony & Reception

Walk last among bridesmaids. Arrange train and veil at altar. Hold both bouquets. Hold groom's ring. Sign marriage license. Bustle the dress (learn at final fitting!). Deliver MOH speech. Ensure bride eats. Handle mini-crises discreetly.

Best Man

Pre-Wedding

Plan bachelor party (match groom's style, coordinate budgets, cover groom's share). Coordinate suit/tux fittings and rentals for all groomsmen. Submit measurements 2-4 months before. Be the go-to for all groomsmen questions.

Day-Of, Ceremony & Reception

Help with ties and boutonnières. Ensure groom eats. Secure wedding rings in inner jacket pocket. Stand next to groom at altar. Present rings at the right moment. Sign marriage license. Give officiant their payment. Deliver best man speech (5-7 min max). Play host — mingle with guests. Distribute tip envelopes.

Bridesmaids

What's Expected

Purchase own dress, shoes, accessories. Attend major events (shower, bachelorette, rehearsal). Contribute to shower and bachelorette costs. Buy shower gift ($50-75) and wedding gift ($100-150). Show up on time. Total cost: $1,200-2,000+ per person.

Day-Of

Help keep bride calm. Answer texts/calls so bride doesn't have to. Walk in designated order. Remain photo-ready. Mingle with guests, encourage dancing. Help pack items at end of night.

Groomsmen

Duties

Collaborate on bachelor party. Obtain attire at own expense. Arrive early to help groom. Usher guests to seats, distribute programs, manage gift table. Help guests find parking and restrooms. Transport gifts home after. Return rented tuxes (including groom's).

Flower Girl & Ring Bearer

Tips & Backup Plans

Best age: 4-6. Rehearsal is critical. Buddy system with older child. Parent at end of aisle as anchor. Best man holds real rings (faux on pillow). Don't force if child refuses — seat them with parents. Accept that a parent may walk alongside.

Parents' Roles

Mother of the Bride

Dress shopping companion. Help coordinate guest list and RSVPs. Help bride get dressed. Processional and receiving line. Parent dance.

Father of the Bride

Walk bride down the aisle. Opening reception toast. Father-daughter dance. Last to leave; settle outstanding vendor bills.

Parents of the Groom

Host and pay for rehearsal dinner. Compile guest list for their side. Mother-son dance. Now contribute ~24% of wedding costs on average.

Coordinator vs. Wedding Party

Coordinator Handles

Timeline, vendor communication, ceremony lineup, reception flow, crisis management, setup and teardown.

Wedding Party Handles

Emotional support, bouquet/rings during ceremony, license signing, speeches, ushering, dance floor energy, keeping couple fed.

No Coordinator? Designate a Point Person

Choose someone NOT in the wedding party. They hold the vendor contact list, know the timeline, make decisions on your behalf. Never the bride, groom, MOH, or best man.

Communicating Expectations

Be Direct, Respectful & Realistic

Put expectations in writing. Ask about budgets before planning. Don't assume people know what's expected. Allow compromises. Don't expect unlimited money, time, or perfection. The friendship should be stronger after the wedding, not damaged by it.
