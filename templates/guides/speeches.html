Speech & Toast Guide

Everything your speakers need to know about timing, structure, and delivery

Print Guide Speeches Dashboard

A well-run speech block is one of the most memorable parts of any wedding reception. This guide covers timing, structure, delivery tips, and etiquette so every speaker can shine without the evening running off schedule.

● Speech Order & Timing

Traditional Order

Best Man → Maid of Honor → Father of the Bride → Father of the Groom → The Couple. The MC or DJ introduces each speaker and keeps the flow moving.

Modern Variations

Brides are speaking more often, mothers are being included, couples are giving joint speeches, and the order is entirely flexible. Do what feels right for your celebration.

Ideal Speech Lengths

Speaker Ideal Length Max
Best Man 3-5 min 7 min
Maid of Honor 3-5 min 7 min
Father of the Bride 5-7 min 9 min
Parents of the Groom 3-5 min 7 min
Couple's Toast 2-3 min 5 min

Total speech block: aim for under 30 minutes.

⚠ What Happens When Speeches Run Long

Audience attention drops sharply after 3 minutes per speaker. Caterers face delays holding hot food. Guests get hungry and restless. Dancing time gets cut short. Keep it tight and your guests will love every word.

● Speech Structure

The 3-Part Framework

Opening (30 seconds - 1 minute)

Introduce yourself briefly, establish your relationship to the couple, and hook the audience with something engaging. You have about 15 seconds to earn their attention.

Body (2-3 minutes)

Share one or two anecdotes that reveal something meaningful about the person you know, then bridge to what you've seen in them as a couple. One great story beats three mediocre ones.

Closing (30 seconds - 1 minute)

Deliver your toast line clearly. Raise your glass toward the couple and invite the room to join you. End with a specific, heartfelt wish and take a sip.

Good Openers

  • A brief, funny observation about the couple or the day
  • "For those who don't know me..." followed by a quick, warm intro
  • A short, engaging anecdote that draws the audience in immediately

Bad Openers

  • "Webster's dictionary defines..." — overused and instantly loses the room
  • "I'm really nervous" — makes the audience nervous too
  • "I didn't prepare anything" — tells guests you don't care enough to try

Ending with a Toast

Give a clear signal: "Please raise your glasses..." Name both partners. Offer a specific wish for their future. Raise your glass toward the couple. Take a sip. That's it — clean and memorable.

● What to Include

Personal Stories That Reveal Character

Choose stories that show who this person really is — their kindness, their humor, their loyalty. One great story told well beats three mediocre ones rushed through.

Humor, Balanced with Sentiment

The best speeches make people laugh and then get a little misty-eyed. Self-deprecating humor works especially well. Test your jokes on someone outside the wedding party to make sure they land.

Compliment Both Partners

Even if you primarily know one half of the couple, make a point to acknowledge and welcome the other. This is about their union, not just your friendship with one of them.

Absent Loved Ones

Get the couple's approval first before mentioning anyone who has passed. Keep the tone uplifting and pivot to how proud they would be. A brief, warm mention is more powerful than a long tribute.

● What to Avoid

  • ✗ Ex-partners — NEVER. Not even as a joke. Not even vaguely.
  • ✗ Embarrassing stories that would mortify parents or grandparents in the room
  • ✗ Inside jokes that nobody outside your friend group understands
  • ✗ Making it about yourself — you are the messenger, not the story
  • ✗ Excessive alcohol before speaking — one drink max before your speech
  • ✗ Reading entirely from your phone — use printed cue cards instead
  • ✗ Winging it — always prepare, even if you are naturally funny
  • ✗ Divorce statistics or cynical marriage jokes — save the dark humor for another occasion

● Tips for Nervous Speakers

Preparation Timeline

  • 4-6 weeks out: Brainstorm stories and themes
  • 3-4 weeks out: Write your first draft
  • 2 weeks out: Edit ruthlessly and test on a trusted friend
  • 1 week out: Finalize cue cards and practice daily
  • Day before: One full run-through, then put it away
  • Day of: One quiet read-through to yourself, then trust your prep

Cue Cards

Use bullet points, not a memorized script and not a full written-out speech. Number your cards. Use large, clear text so you can glance down and look back up quickly. Give a backup copy to a friend in case you misplace yours.

Breathing Techniques

Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)

Inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4. Repeat 3-4 times.

Extended Exhale (4-4-8)

Inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4, exhale slowly for 8. This activates the parasympathetic nervous system and calms you quickly. Practice 5-10 minutes before your speech.

Where to Look

Scan left, center, and right — don't fixate on one spot. Find 3-5 friendly, smiling faces in different parts of the room and rotate between them. During the heartfelt moments, look directly at the couple. They are your anchor.

● Toast Etiquette for Guests

Raising Your Glass

Only raise your glass when the speaker invites you to. Hold it by the stem. Raise it toward the couple.

The Couple Being Toasted

The couple being toasted remains seated and does not drink to themselves. They smile, acknowledge the toast, and can take a sip after the room has finished.

Non-Drinkers

Absolutely participate in the toast — raise whatever you have. Water, juice, soda, sparkling cider. Any beverage is acceptable. The gesture is what matters, not the contents of the glass.

When During the Reception

Toasts work best between courses (after the main course, before dessert) or after dinner before dancing begins. This gives guests time to settle in with drinks and keeps the energy flowing into the next phase of the evening.
