Vendor Etiquette & Tipping Guide

Who to tip, how much, when to deliver, and day-of vendor communication

Print Guide Tips Tracker Dashboard

Tipping wedding vendors can feel confusing. This guide covers standard tipping ranges, when tips are and aren't expected, how to organize envelope distribution, and best practices for working with vendors on your wedding day.

● When Tipping Is NOT Expected

Business Owners vs. Employees

Owners set their own pricing and profit margins. The convention of tipping applies primarily to employees who don't control their rates. If the person who owns the business is also your day-of vendor, a tip is a generous gesture but not an expectation.

Service Charge vs. Gratuity — NOT the Same Thing

A "service charge" is a fee that may go to the company, not the staff. A "gratuity" goes directly to the workers. Always ask your vendor: "Does the service charge go to the staff?" If it doesn't, consider tipping the staff separately.

Tips Are Never Expected For:

Brief deliveries (dropping off rentals, linens, etc.). Poor or unprofessional service — address the issue instead. When your budget doesn't allow it — heartfelt thank-you notes, glowing online reviews, and referrals to friends are meaningful alternatives that cost nothing.

● How to Deliver Tips

The Labeled Envelope System

Withdraw all tip cash at least 2 weeks before the wedding. Prepare individual envelopes labeled with each vendor's name and a brief handwritten thank-you note. Seal each envelope and organize them in a box or folder in the order they'll be distributed throughout the day.

The "Tip Distributor"

Designate ONE person to handle all tip distribution: your planner, maid of honor, or best man. Provide them with a checklist that includes each vendor's name, their envelope, and the timing for delivery. The couple should never be fumbling for envelopes on their wedding day.

● Day-Of Vendor Communication

Establish a Single Point of Contact

On the wedding day, your planner becomes the sole point of contact for all vendor questions and logistics. If you don't have a planner, designate a trusted friend or family member as your vendor liaison. The couple should NOT be fielding vendor questions about setup, timing, or logistics on their wedding day.

Vendor Meals

Any vendor working 4-6+ hours during meal service should receive a hot meal. Serve vendor meals after the couple but before the remaining guest tables. Provide a private or semi-private dining area so vendors can take a genuine break. This is not just etiquette — well-fed vendors perform better.

Timeline & Confirmation Protocol

Share the final detailed timeline with all vendors 3-4 weeks before the wedding. Make a confirmation call or send a confirmation email to every vendor 1 week before the event to reconfirm arrival times, setup details, and any last-minute changes.

● When Things Go Wrong

Address Immediately If:

There is a safety issue (food allergy concern, electrical problem, structural hazard). Contracted services are not being delivered as agreed. The problem will worsen if left unaddressed (e.g., sound system failing, wrong table arrangement before guests arrive). Have your planner or liaison handle these — never the couple.

Let It Go If:

It's a minor aesthetic issue that only you would notice (slightly different shade of napkin, one centerpiece shorter than the others). It's a small timing delay that won't cascade into larger problems. The imperfection won't show up in photos or affect your guests' experience.

Post-Wedding: Handling Vendor Issues

Wait 2-3 weeks after the wedding before reaching out about any issues — emotions will have settled and you'll communicate more clearly. Contact the vendor directly before posting public reviews. Be factual and specific about what happened versus what was contracted. Most reputable vendors will work to make it right.

Overall Tipping Budget Guidance

Budget 5-10% of your total wedding cost for vendor tips. For most weddings, the total tipping amount falls between $500 and $1,500. Build this into your budget from the start so it doesn't come as a surprise at the end.
