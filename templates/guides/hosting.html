Hosting & Thank-You Etiquette

Making guests feel welcome and expressing gratitude before, during, and after your wedding

Print Guide Day-Of Timeline Dashboard

Hospitality is the thread that runs through your entire wedding weekend. From the moment guests arrive to the thank-you notes you send afterward, every detail is a chance to show the people you love how much their presence means to you.

● Pre-Wedding Hospitality

Welcome Bags

Include water, local snacks, a weekend itinerary, a hangover kit, and a handwritten note. Deliver bags to the hotel in time for guest check-in. Plan one bag per room or per couple, not per person.

Welcome Party

Host the evening before the wedding, keeping it to 2-3 hours. Everyone invited to the wedding should be invited to the welcome party. Keep the format casual: appetizers and drinks are sufficient. This is not a gift occasion.

Rehearsal Dinner

Traditionally hosted by the groom's family. You must invite: wedding party and their partners, the officiant and their partner, parents and grandparents, and siblings. The rehearsal dinner should never be more formal than the wedding itself.

● Day-Of Guest Experience

Ceremony Programs

Include the order of ceremony, the wedding party list, a thank-you message, and an optional "In Loving Memory" section. Sample wording: "We are so grateful you are here to share this day with us. Your love and support mean more than words can say. Thank you for being part of our story."

Signage

Prepare signs for parking, welcome, directional arrows, restrooms, seating chart, and an unplugged ceremony notice. Clear signage prevents confusion and reduces the number of questions your wedding party has to field.

Dietary Needs

Add a dietary needs field to your RSVP. Label all buffet items with common indicators: GF (gluten-free), V (vegetarian), VG (vegan), DF (dairy-free), NF (nut-free). Brief your catering staff on all dietary restrictions. For plated meals, use coded place cards so servers can deliver the correct dish without asking.

Accessibility

Ensure wheelchair-accessible paths to all key areas. Remove chairs at tables where wheelchair users will sit. Hire a sign language interpreter if needed. Use high-contrast fonts on all signage and printed materials. Add an accommodations request field to your RSVP so guests can communicate needs in advance.

● Post-Wedding Thank-You Notes

Timeline

When to Send Thank-You Notes

Pre-wedding gifts: within 2 weeks of receiving them. Shower hosts: within 2 days of the shower. Wedding gifts: within 3 months of your wedding (not a year, despite the common myth). Set a daily goal of writing 3-4 notes to stay on track without burning out.

The Formula

Four Parts of a Great Thank-You Note

Start with a warm greeting. Mention the specific gift. Make a personal connection (how you plan to use it, a memory from the wedding). Close with a warm sign-off. This formula keeps notes sincere and specific without overthinking.

By Gift Type

Registry Gift

Name the specific item and describe how you will use it. Example: "Thank you so much for the beautiful stand mixer. We have already used it twice and our Sunday mornings will never be the same."

Cash Gift

Mention your plans for the money but never state the amount. Example: "Your incredibly generous gift is going toward our honeymoon trip to Portugal, and we cannot wait to explore Lisbon thanks to your kindness."

Group Gift

Each contributor receives their own individual thank-you note. Never send a single group thank-you, even if multiple people went in on the same gift.

No Gift

Focus on their attendance and what it meant to you. Example: "Having you there to celebrate with us made the day so special. Your presence truly was the best gift."

Off-Registry Gift

Show that you appreciate the thought behind the gift. Reference what makes the item special or unique, and describe how it will fit into your life together.

Who Else to Thank

Beyond Gift-Givers

Parents: for their support, love, and any financial contributions. Wedding party: pair a thank-you note with an attendant gift. Exceptional vendors: a thoughtful note goes a long way and helps their business. Anyone who helped with logistics: DIY helpers, day-of coordinators, friends who went above and beyond.

Format

Always Handwritten

Thank-you notes should always be handwritten. No fill-in-the-blank cards, no emails, and no social media posts as substitutes. A handwritten note shows genuine effort and personal care that no other format can match.

● Guest Favors

Keep It Simple

Favors are entirely optional. Edible favors work best: cookies, honey, coffee, or local treats. Avoid overly personalized items (monogrammed koozies, custom bottle openers) as guests rarely keep them.

Donation in Lieu of Favors

Choose a cause that is meaningful to you as a couple. Place one discreet sign on the favor table letting guests know. Keep the donation amount private. Do not say "in honor of our guests" as this can feel presumptuous.

When Favors Are Not Needed

You are already providing a generous experience with a beautiful celebration, food, drinks, and entertainment. If your budget is tight, favors are an easy place to save. Intimate weddings with fewer than 30 guests rarely need formal favors.
